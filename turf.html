Mid-South Sod and Turf Imaging

Find the patches before the block ships.

Aerial imaging for sod farms across the Mid-South. I fly a block, mark the spots that look different from the turf around them, and hand you the picture before harvest. A local pilot, not a subscription.

What it is

Same idea as an X-ray. The imagery shows where the turf looks different and where to go look. Your farm manager or superintendent reads the cause and decides what to do. I fly, process, and hand you a clear picture. You make the call.

I don't grade turf quality, and I don't name a weed or a disease. I mark the spot.

What you get back

The report comes back within 48 hours of landing. Weather can move the flight day. It doesn't move the clock.

The whole block in one picture

A sharp true-color image of the entire block, stitched from the flight, so you can look it over without walking every yard.

Turf vigor image

A color image showing which parts of the block are running behind the turf around them on the day of the flight. It is relative to that block, not a calibrated measurement.

Patches marked and lettered

Thin, off-color, or uneven patches get a letter and a close-up. Spot locations are approximate, good for walking your crew to them.

A short written summary

Plain words on what stood out and where. It says where to look, not what the problem is.

A dated record

Fly the same block again and each marked patch is in the same place, so you can see whether it is still there, bigger, or gone.

Files that open on a phone

A report, the images, and a Google Earth layer. No platform to log into and no subscription.

Start with one block

One visit, at a flat price I quote before I come. Pick a block you plan to cut soon. If the picture shows you nothing you didn't already know, you have spent one small job finding that out.

Bermuda and zoysia are flown after spring green-up. Fescue can be flown in winter.
